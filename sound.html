Swimming in sound.

Lie down, close your eyes and listen to what lives beneath the noise.

Book your place

Listen

The mind gets loud. Sound is the way through.

For around 75 minutes you lie still while singing bowls, drums and the sounds of nature move through the room. There's no technique to learn and nothing you need to get right. The sound does the work. All you have to do is listen, and let it carry you somewhere quieter.

How it works

Slow sound has a way of slowing everything else down with it. Your breath begins to follow the long tones, your body softens, and your nervous system shifts out of alert and into rest. Eventually the mind stops chasing thoughts and starts following sound instead, and you find yourself in meditation without ever having tried to meditate.

Sound, silence, and the space between.

As old as humans

People have been using sound to journey inward for thousands of years. In India, the tradition of Nada Yoga teaches that listening itself is a path to meditation, and that beneath every sound we hear lies a deeper one, the unstruck sound, waiting to be noticed. None of this is new. What is new is how much we need it.

The instruments

Crystal quartz singing bowls, a contrabass drum, kalimbas, rainsticks and wind chimes, carried by recordings from nature. Each carries its own tone and texture, and together they create a soundscape that feels both ancient and entirely of this moment.
